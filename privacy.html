Who we are

Thaki is offered by Tural Mustafayev, an individual developer in Baku, Azerbaijan, who decides how your data is used (the "controller"). You can reach me about anything in this policy at mustafayev.t@outlook.com.

Effective 19 October 2026, version 2.

The short version

Your goals live on your phone unless you sign in. If you sign in, they sync to our server. If you ask Thaki to plan a goal, and only after you agree on a sheet that tells you so, its title and a few of your answers go to a third-party AI service to draft that plan, and nothing else does. We show no ads and track you across no apps.

If you never sign in and never ask Thaki to plan, your goals, milestones, tiny steps and journal never leave your phone. What does leave it then: usage events, which you can switch off (You → Share usage stats), and the requests the app makes to RevenueCat to show Thaki Pro's prices and to handle a purchase.

Planning with Thaki

Planning is optional. It is a Thaki Pro feature and needs an account. Building your plan yourself sends nothing.

Who gets your data. When you agree and ask Thaki to plan a goal, our server sends the request to OpenRouter, a third-party AI service in the United States. OpenRouter passes it to a third-party AI model run by a model provider it picks, and that provider writes the plan. Neither OpenRouter nor the providers are part of Thaki.

What is sent: the goal as you typed it; your answers about how much time you have each day, how often you want a tiny step (your pace) and what usually gets in the way; and, if you have already finished a goal, how many days a week you walked it and about how many tiny steps each milestone held (as bands, not dates). Nothing else is added to the request. What is never sent: your name, your email, your other goals, your journal, your contacts or your location. The request carries no account id, email address or IP address of yours.

Keeping and training. Each request asks OpenRouter to route it only to model providers that keep no copy and don't train on it (OpenRouter's zero-data-retention and no-data-collection settings). We rely on OpenRouter and the providers to honour that; we can't inspect their systems, and we can't promise how they handle data once they have it. OpenRouter's own policy (openrouter.ai/privacy) says how it handles requests.

Crisis check. Before anything is sent, the app and our server check the goal's title for words that suggest a crisis. If they find them, nothing goes to the AI service and the app shows where to find help.

Your agreement. Before your first plan, Thaki shows a short sheet, titled "Let Thaki plan" and your goal's name, that says what is sent and that it goes to a third-party AI service. Nothing is sent until you tap "Agree and plan"; "Not now" sends nothing. Signing in is not agreement. You are asked once per account, and we keep your agreement on your account, with this policy's version and the date, so a second phone signed in to the same account doesn't ask again. If we change what planning sends, we change this policy's version and ask you again before your next plan.

Plans from Thaki are suggestions generated automatically. They can be wrong or unsuitable, and you can change or delete anything in them.

Withdrawing. Stop tapping Let Thaki plan it: nothing is sent unless you tap it. To remove the record of your agreement and everything else on our server, delete your account, or write to us. Withdrawing doesn't affect plans you already have.

As the table in "What we collect" says. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you can ask us to:

  • tell you what we hold about you, and give you a copy (as a file you can take elsewhere);
  • correct it;
  • delete it;
  • restrict how we use it;
  • stop using it for something we do on the basis of legitimate interest (for usage events, switch off You → Share usage stats);
  • withdraw a consent you gave, without affecting what we did before.

Write to mustafayev.t@outlook.com. If you use Sign in with Apple, say so and we will tell you how to confirm it's you. We answer within one month, or sooner where your local law sets a shorter deadline.

You have the right to complain to the data-protection authority where you live: in the EU, your country's supervisory authority; in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO); in Japan, the Personal Information Protection Commission (PPC); in South Korea, the Personal Information Protection Commission (PIPC). We'd be glad to try to help first.

Deleting your account

You → the profile card → Account → Delete account. Your account and everything on our server go; the goals on your phone stay. Thaki Pro is billed by Apple and keeps renewing until you cancel it in your Apple Account.

When you delete your account we also ask Apple to revoke Thaki's access if you used Sign in with Apple, delete your record at RevenueCat, remove your IP address and account id from plan usage records, and remove your account id from usage events. To remove the goals on your phone too, delete the app.

If you never signed in, nothing on our server is linked to you. Usage events from your phone are deleted after 180 days, and you can stop them with You → Share usage stats.

Children

Thaki is for people 13 and older (14 in South Korea).

If a parent or guardian withdraws consent for a child's use of the app through Apple, Apple tells us and we keep that notice so we can act on it.

If you believe a child under 13 uses Thaki, write to us and we will delete the account.

No ads, no tracking

Thaki contains no advertising software and never reads your device's advertising identifier (IDFA). We don't sell your data or share it for advertising, and we don't track you across other companies' apps or websites.

Because we don't track you, there is nothing for a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal to switch off; we treat both as already honoured.

Security

Every connection between the app and our services is encrypted (TLS). On our server, row-level security means an account can reach only its own data. On your phone, your sign-in is kept in the iOS Keychain.

Changes

If we make a material change to this policy, we will tell you in the app before it takes effect, and ask for your consent again where the law requires it. Each version has a number and an effective date; earlier versions stay available on request.
